# The Lull: the story (final)

**FINAL storyboard v1.0 (astronaut; comedy base + the three judges' grafts), 2026-10-23, storyboard only, for Alex's review.** Shot by shot: `SHOTLIST.md` (`shotlist.json`). The actor: `CAST.md`. Every line on screen against the references, with reading rates: `COPY.md`. Beat by beat against higgsfield01: `VS-HF01.md`. Nothing has been generated: no image, video, audio or network calls.

**Logline.** Mid-spacewalk, an astronaut gets Marketing's email: the launch moved up, the film is due tonight. With five deadpan prompts from her wrist display she has Manus 2.0 recast every disaster that follows (a tether that runs out, a debris shower in a solar storm, an airlock that will not seal, a fireball re-entry) as a serene launch film for the station's new guest module, and it ships while she is in radio blackout. Then, bobbing on the ocean, she gets the next brief.

## The story

1. At 09:12, on a spacewalk at the end of the station's truss, Commander Wren Adair gets an email from Marketing, surfaced by her Manus Automation: the launch moved up, and they need the launch film for The Lull, the station's new guest module, tonight.
2. She is already up; she lets the wrench in her glove drift away, rips open her wrist display, and while her thoughts float off in zero G ('Tonight?' 'From space?' 'Nose itches.'), an orbital sunrise blasts her visor, and she has her opening shot.
3. She starts the job herself with one rule ('Lull launch film. 30s. Make it calm.'), and Manus 2.0 builds it in Studio's Video Editor from her own helmet-cam footage, with Alchemy setting the direction and, as we depict it (check with Manus), her always-on Cloud Computer doing the work.
4. Every disaster that follows tests the rule, and every time she answers with a calm creative note that turns the disaster into luxury: drifting off the hull on a tether that is running out, she sends 'Open on this drift.' a second before it snaps taut and yanks her out of frame.
5. On the night side, a debris shower chases her hand over hand along the truss over an aurora as a solar storm arrives; she sends 'Call it shooting stars.' and shouts 'Wish fast!', and five seconds later the film plays her debris, slowed and warm, under 'Wish slowly.'
6. The storm drives her into an airlock whose hatch will not close on her own tether, the radiation alarm pulsing red, and the wrench she lost this morning comes back around and clangs off the rim an inch above her helmet; she watches it go, blinks, and types 'Red alarm? Mood lighting.'
7. Hours later she is going home: the capsule burns into the atmosphere, plasma at the window, the plush whale swinging, her signal falling bar by bar, and on the last bar she sends 'End on this glow. Ship it.'
8. Then loss of signal; the film finishes and ships to Marketing without her, on her Cloud Computer (as we depict it: check with Manus), while she is unreachable.
9. At 19:47 she is sitting on top of the capsule in a golden sea, helmet off, when the reply comes in ('Booked solid.') and then a follow-up ('Undersea next?').
10. She looks down at the water under her boots; the camera pulls back on a great deal of sea; the music stops; a gull lands on the capsule; the end card reads 'Out of office. / Out of range. / Still shipping.'

**Shape.** One working day, one antagonist: space itself, escalating all day. Sunrise blast (S04) -> a tether that runs out (S09, comic peril) -> a debris shower in a solar storm (S14, a chase) -> trapped in an airlock that will not seal, radiation alarm, a near-miss above the helmet (S20-S22) -> re-entry fireball and radio blackout (S24, the peak) -> a calm golden sea (S26). The danger peaks on loop 5, as hf01's does; the release comes only after the file is sent.

## The re-entry blackout ending

S24 is the film's peak and its product truth. The capsule's plasma sheath blocks radio (real), so the signal glyph in her prompt bar falls bar by bar: two, one, none, one, none, one. She types P5 early and then waits, utterly calm while everything rattles, for one bar; she sends on it at n1895 (hf01's send frame); the antenna glyph is struck through at n1897. Nothing in her film is shot after that: 'this glow' is her own clip, recorded before the send. S25 cuts from 'no signal' straight to the file card: The Lull, her glow as the thumbnail, a cloud icon, a blue check. It finished on her always-on Cloud Computer and went to Marketing while she was out of range. The re-entry roar is the film's first loud real sound; the waves at splashdown are its first natural sound.

## The punchline

S26, at 19:47 on the sea: 'Booked solid.' (her spa film of her disasters sold out the hotel module), then, 0.4 s later, a second bubble: 'Undersea next?' The next brief is about the world she is in now, as hf01's is, and it restarts the loop: she is sitting on the sea, and Marketing wants what is under it. Her smile fades into a slow look down at the water; the camera pulls back on an enormous sea; the bubbles clear, the music stops, and a gull lands on the capsule. Ours, not hf01's: no praise word, no 'Next brief:', no safety film.

## The end-card line

Out of office. / Out of range. / Still shipping. ('shipping.' in #33ABFB with a hand-drawn blue underline; S27 only.) 'Out of office' now means three things: the auto-reply idiom, a spacewalk outside the station, and the orbit she has just left. 'Out of range.' is the blackout. 'Still shipping.' is Manus, set up by P5's 'Ship it.' Fallback: the brief's two lines.
