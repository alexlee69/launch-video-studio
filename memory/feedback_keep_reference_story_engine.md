On 2026-10-23 Alex turned down the first Manus 2.0 story, "Out of office": a founder on a foggy ridge run makes an ad for her own shoe. His words: "our story seems more boring, just a founder making an ad for her own product". He picked the alternative, an astronaut on a spacewalk with a re-entry blackout ending and a "next brief" punchline.

**Why:** higgsfield01 works because of four things:
- **A heightened genre:** an Indiana Jones pastiche.
- **A running joke:** a desk job inside an action movie, so he opens a laptop under fire.
- **Set pieces in every loop:** monkeys, an arrow, a boulder.
- **A punchline ending:** "Next brief: …".

It even already ties the deliverable to the world: the promo is for the reserve he runs through. My process killed the fun:
- the premise was locked early from a features-first pitch option;
- the writers could only vary the setting;
- the judges scored fidelity, feasibility, brand and copy safety, never excitement or humour.

**How to apply:**
- When a story recreates a reference, keep the reference's story engine and change the genre and the world. A nouns-only swap is still the copy trap.
- Let writers vary the premise and tone, not just the location.
- Always include a judge who scores excitement and humour against the reference.
- Show Alex the logline and premise options before a full storyboard.
